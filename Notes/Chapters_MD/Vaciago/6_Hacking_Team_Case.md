## Introduction

The Hacking Team case connects offensive cyber capabilities, human rights, export controls, trade secrets, and digital search and seizure. It also serves as the basis for a lab exercise on forensic consultancy in a dispute over alleged spyware source-code transfer.

---

## 1. Company and Technology

Hacking Team was founded in Milan in 2003 and sold offensive surveillance software to governments and law enforcement.

Its tools included Remote Control System, mobile surveillance, remote activation of microphones and cameras, stealth functions, battery optimisation, and data extraction.

---

## 2. Ethical and Legal Issues

The case raises concerns about:
- sales to non-democratic or high-risk governments;
- human-rights abuses;
- privacy violations;
- lack of clear export blacklists for offensive cyber tools;
- the boundary between lawful investigation and unlawful malware.

Vaciago stresses that states may need lawful offensive tools, but private export for profit creates serious regulatory risk.

---

## 3. Data Breach and Fallout

In 2015, Hacking Team was breached and a large archive of internal data was leaked. The leak exposed clients, operations, and sales. Vaciago uses the case to underline that even ethically motivated hacking can create severe real-world consequences.

---

## 4. SoftHack and Trade Secret Allegation

The lab scenario focuses on SoftHack Srl, a Turin company linked to former Hacking Team employees. A payment from a Saudi company was suspected of masking transfer of Galileo/RCS source code.

Students must argue either that SoftHack stole and transferred trade secrets or that the accusation is unsupported.

---

## 5. Search and Seizure

Vaciago mentions search and seizure on the Turin-based company as the procedural setting for the lab. Students must reason from either side of the allegation and decide what technical consultancy would support or challenge the source-code-transfer theory.

---

## Key Concepts & Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **RCS** | Remote Control System, Hacking Team's surveillance platform. |
| **Offensive Cyber Tool** | Software designed to intrude into, monitor, or control target systems. |
| **Trade Secret Disclosure** | Unauthorised transfer or exposure of confidential business or technical information. |
| **Search and Seizure** | Procedural collection of evidence under legal authorisation. |

---

## Summary
- Hacking Team sold offensive surveillance technologies internationally.
- The case highlights the need for legal controls on commercial spyware.
- Offensive tools can support lawful investigations but also human-rights abuses.
- The 2015 breach revealed internal operations and controversial clients.
- The SoftHack allegation concerns possible source-code transfer connected to a Saudi payment.
- The lab requires prosecution or defence reasoning through a forensic expert report.
- The lab requires students to connect technical evidence with the legal theory of trade-secret disclosure.
